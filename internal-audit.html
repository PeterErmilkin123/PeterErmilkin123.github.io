Internal Audit Simulation

An introduction to identifying risks, evaluating controls, and using evidence to explain where business processes can improve.

Completed: September 2026Tools: Excel & scenario analysisContext: Educational simulation

What I worked on

I completed a Goldman Sachs Internal Audit job simulation through Forage. The exercises introduced audit planning, risk assessment, internal controls, reporting, and follow-up.

I practiced using a probability-and-impact matrix in an Excel workbook and reviewed three simulated Global Banking & Markets scenarios involving operational, credit, and compliance risks.

Applying a risk mindset

The risk assessment workbook applies audit concepts to an exam preparation plan. It identifies problems such as delayed studying, competing deadlines, knowledge gaps, and distractions.

Each entry connects the risk to a cause and consequence, assigns probability and impact, and proposes a practical control. For example, delayed studying receives a critical rating, with scheduled study blocks and daily progress checks proposed as controls.

Three areas of analysis

The figures below come from the learning scenarios. They are illustrative case data, not reported Goldman Sachs operating results or current regulatory benchmarks.

01 / Operational risk

Trade execution

The scenario reported discrepancies in 150 of 2,000 trades, a 7.5% discrepancy rate. Average execution took five seconds against the scenario’s three-second comparison point. The exercise connected process delays with financial losses and client service.

02 / Credit risk

Corporate lending

The scenario’s non-performing loan ratio increased from 4.5% to 7.5%, with mid-sized companies accounting for a disproportionate share of problem loans. The exercise highlighted credit assessment and ongoing borrower monitoring.

03 / Compliance risk

Anti-money-laundering controls

Only 30 of 50 identified suspicious transactions were reported in the scenario. Incomplete employee training and recurring audit findings highlighted the importance of escalation, follow-up, and control monitoring.

What I learned

Internal audit involves more than finding mistakes. It requires assessing the significance of a risk, connecting observations to evidence, and explaining how a control could address the underlying problem.

The simulation helped me practice attention to detail, risk prioritization, and clear communication—skills I can apply in accounting coursework and future work.
